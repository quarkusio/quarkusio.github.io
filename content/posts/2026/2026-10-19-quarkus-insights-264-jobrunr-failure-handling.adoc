== Origins and Architecture of JobRunr

Ronald shared the backstory behind JobRunr. While freelancing prior to the pandemic, he faced distributed computing challenges in Java where background tasks lacked consistent correlation IDs, jobs vanished into dead-letter queues without monitoring, and debugging required parsing disjointed logs.

Frustrated by the lack of developer-friendly tooling, Ronald spent eight months building JobRunr from the ground up. To make the project commercially sustainable, he introduced a Pro tier alongside the open source core, which has since grown into a full-time business supporting a dedicated five-person team.

Architecturally, JobRunr allows developers to enqueue standard Java 8+ lambdas directly from their services or REST endpoints. Rather than executing heavy tasks synchronously on the frontend server, JobRunr serializes the invocation and persists it immediately into a shared database (such as https://www.postgresql.org/[PostgreSQL]). Dedicated background worker nodes continuously poll the database, lock pending jobs, and execute the work asynchronously. This design keeps web endpoints fast and lightweight while allowing background processing nodes to scale independently on container platforms like https://www.redhat.com/en/technologies/cloud-computing/openshift[Red Hat OpenShift].

== Carbon-Aware Job Scheduling

Both Holly and Ronald share a passion for green software engineering and sustainability. In JobRunr, Ronald introduced carbon-aware scheduling to help developers minimize the environmental impact of their compute workloads.

When scheduling batch jobs, developers often do not need immediate execution as long as the task completes before a specific deadline. With carbon-aware scheduling, developers specify their geographic location (such as Belgium) and a target execution window (for example, between now and four hours from now).

Behind the scenes, JobRunr evaluates real-time energy grid data and forecasts:

* **Grid intensity forecasting:** In Europe, JobRunr connects to the https://www.entsoe.eu/[ENTSO-E] (European Network of Transmission System Operators for Electricity) API to forecast when grid carbon emissions are lowest due to renewable generation (wind and solar).
* **North American grid support:** Because United States power grids are fragmented across regional operators, JobRunr partnered with https://www.electricitymaps.com/[Electricity Maps] to provide grid carbon intensity forecasts across North America.
* **Fallback behavior:** If grid forecasting APIs become unavailable, JobRunr safely schedules the job at the end of the requested window to maximize the chance of acquiring fresh grid data before executing.
* **Real-world adoption:** Enterprise users such as sports retailer https://www.decathlon.com/[Decathlon] utilize carbon-aware scheduling to process batch data during low-emission hours while guaranteeing completion before retail stores open each morning.

The JobRunr dashboard provides visual graphs mapping diurnal grid patterns, identifying solar peaks, morning coffee surges, and optimal low-carbon execution windows.

== Handling Job Failures Halfway: Step-Level Idempotency with `JobContext`

The central focus of JobRunr 9 is addressing what happens when a complex, multi-step job fails partway through execution.

Consider an e-commerce order fulfillment workflow:

1. Charge the customer credit card via https://stripe.com/[Stripe].
2. Generate an invoice PDF document.
3. Send the invoice email via https://sendgrid.com/[SendGrid].

By default, JobRunr automatically retries failed jobs up to 10 times using exponential backoff. If the third step fails (for example, if SendGrid experiences a temporary outage), a naive retry executes the entire method again from the beginning. This risks charging the customer multiple times for the same order.

To solve this problem, JobRunr 9 introduces `JobContext` and the `runStepOnce(...)` construct:

* **Step recording:** Each distinct phase of the job is wrapped in a `jobContext.runStepOnce(...)` block.
* **Automatic step skipping:** When a step succeeds, JobRunr records its completion in the job's state metadata.
* **Safe retries:** If a subsequent step throws an exception, JobRunr retries only the failed step and any remaining steps. Previously completed steps (such as the payment processing and invoice generation) are automatically skipped.
* **Timeline visualization:** The JobRunr dashboard renders a real-time visual timeline of the job execution, marking completed steps in green, failed steps in red, and skipped steps clearly during retries.

== Open Source vs. Pro and Workflow Orchestration

Ronald clarified the differences between the open source and commercial Pro editions of JobRunr:
